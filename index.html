Data sources & methodology

This map shows two different things: official police statistics per district (Layer A) and individual media reports (Layer B). Neither is a guarantee about safety, and neither names or identifies victims.

Layer A – district colours (official statistics)

  • What: cases registered by police in calendar year 2024 for the chosen crime head (also 2022 and 2023 rape counts in the district popup).
  • Source: National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB), Crime in India 2024, district-wise “Crime against Women” data. NCRB publishes district-wise data as unnumbered “District Wise Reports” (see the NCRB additional tables page; the 2023/2024 district files were not listed there when checked on 10 Oct 2026). The numbers and district boundaries used here come from the Esri India Living Atlas layer “NCRB/District_Wise_Crime_Against_Women_2022” (fields such as totrape24, totmod24, totcrwom24).
  • Cross-check: summing the districts gives 29,441 rape cases for 2024 vs 29,536 in NCRB Crime in India 2024 Vol. I Table 3A.2 (99.7%); Rajasthan 4,871 vs 4,871; Maharashtra 3,091 vs 3,091; Madhya Pradesh 3,061 vs 3,061; Telangana 1,148 vs 1,148. 2022 sum 31,438 vs 31,516 published. Small gaps are police units not tied to a district (e.g. railway/GRP, some Delhi units).
  • Colours: all districts with data are split into three equal-sized groups (Low/Medium/High) for the chosen category and measure; this is a relative ranking, not an absolute danger rating. More cases can mean a bigger population or better reporting.
  • Per lakh women: cases ÷ Census 2011 female population × 100,000 (Census 2011 is the latest census). Districts created after 2011 show no rate; split districts may be understated.

Layer B – incident markers (media reports)

  • Collected from: Google News RSS searches (English & Hindi) and public X (Twitter) posts. Each marker links to the original article(s)/post(s) and shows the outlet and publish date. Items whose only source was an aggregated X news-story summary are not shown (no verifiable link).
  • Place: the exact place words found in the headline/post (shown as “place as reported”) are matched to a district/city list; a locality or police-station name is used only if OpenStreetMap Nominatim finds it within 40 km of that city. Pins are approximate: ±10–30 km (city/district) or ±2–5 km (locality). Pins never mark an exact address.
  • Type & status: assigned automatically from keywords in the headline/post (e.g. “arrested/गिरफ्तार” → arrest reported). Reports of the same type and city within ~2 days are grouped as one incident. Errors are possible; always read the linked report.
  • Dates: “report date” = publish date of the first source; “first seen” = when this map first collected it. Data is cumulative (old incidents are kept) and updated daily.
  • Privacy: no victim names, ages, photos or identifying details are stored or shown (BNS Sec. 72). X post text is not reproduced.

Limits

Media covers only a small, uneven fraction of crimes (more Hindi-belt coverage). No markers or a green district does not mean an area is safe. Emergency: 112 · Women helpline: 181.

Disclaimer

Yeh data adhoora hai (this data is incomplete). NCRB counts only crimes registered with police; many crimes are never reported. News/social-media markers cover only a small sample of recent reports picked up by automated search, and their locations are approximate (city/district or police-station area — never exact addresses).
No markers or a green district does NOT mean an area is safe. Higher counts can also reflect larger population or better reporting. Social-media items are unverified. Use this as awareness only, not as a guarantee. No victim identities are stored or shown (BNS Sec. 72).

Sources (full methodology)

• District data: NCRB, Crime in India 2024 (district-wise Crime Against Women tables; also 2022 & 2023), via Esri India Living Atlas layer “NCRB/District_Wise_Crime_Against_Women_2022” (link). District boundaries from the same layer.
• *Rate uses Census 2011 female population (latest census; districts created after 2011 or split since show no rate, and split parents may be under-stated).
• Incidents: Google News RSS search (English & Hindi) and public X posts / X news stories, collected . Locations geocoded with OpenStreetMap Nominatim.
